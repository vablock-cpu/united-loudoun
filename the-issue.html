What's Being Proposed

An 8.3-Mile Industrial Corridor Through Our Neighborhoods

Dominion Energy is seeking approval from the State Corporation Commission to build a new high-voltage transmission line called "Golden to Mars" — named after two substations. The line would cut through residential Loudoun County — including Loudoun Valley Estates I and II — past multiple schools and parks, and run within feet of hundreds of homes.

Why This Is Happening Now

PJM Interconnection, the regional grid operator, approved the Golden to Mars project in December 2023. Dominion then filed for a Certificate of Public Convenience and Necessity (CPCN) from the Virginia State Corporation Commission. The SCC hearing concluded after 11+ days of testimony — and a ruling is expected any day.

What the SCC decides will stand for generations. Once towers are built in a residential neighborhood, they stay. There is no undo button.

The SCC record shows that Dominion knew about the need for this line as early as 2022 — but filed its application in a way that left no time to properly study underground alternatives. SCC Staff called this out directly: Dominion "effectively ensured that its overhead routes are the only viable options."

ROUTE 1F EXPOSED

Route 1F: A Threat, Not a Plan

Dominion introduced Route 1F at the SCC hearing with no prefiled testimony, no engineering analysis, and no VDOT approval. Their own counsel later admitted it is "not under consideration." It exists on paper as leverage — to pressure the community into accepting Routes 3 and 4.

Dominion's Own Feasibility Study

Dominion commissioned a Feasibility Analysis (v6) for Route 1F along Loudoun County Parkway. Their own study found that underground construction through this stretch is "not constructable" — the median is too narrow, the utilities too dense, and the disruption too severe.

But then Dominion proposed overhead routing through the exact same corridor — 185-foot monopoles with 56-foot crossarms in a median that is 16 to 22 feet wide mid-block and narrows to 4 to 6 feet at intersections. The crossarm alone exceeds the median width by a factor of ten at the narrowest points.

If underground won't fit, overhead won't either. The physical constraints don't change based on the type of structure. This route was never engineered to be built — it was engineered to be feared.

Based on Dominion’s own filings and physical site conditions along Loudoun County Parkway.

What Route 1F Would Actually Hit

Foundation Requirements

Each 185-foot monopole requires an 8-to-12-foot-diameter caisson drilled 30 to 50 feet deep — into a median packed with water, sewer, and fiber utilities. Dominion's own study acknowledges this would require "significant service disruption and relocations" to existing infrastructure serving tens of thousands of residents.

Based on Dominion’s own structure specifications and VDOT median measurements.

Inova HealthPlex Emergency Center

Route 1F would place 500kV transmission structures within 35 feet of the Inova Ashburn HealthPlex — an emergency room that serves as the primary emergency stabilization point for over 280,000 residents (62% of Loudoun County's population). The facility's CT scanner already operates at 111.72% of state-standard capacity. 500kV lines generate electromagnetic fields that do not attenuate to the 0.5 milligauss threshold required for diagnostic imaging until 700 to 1,000 feet from the conductor centerline. The HealthPlex is 35 feet away. Image artifacts from interference — visual noise or blurring — are capable of masking a brain bleed or clot in a stroke patient who depends on speed and precision for survival.

Based on Inova HealthPlex operational data and EMF attenuation standards for diagnostic imaging.

Medevac Operations

The HealthPlex campus includes a ground-level medevac landing zone designated by Loudoun County EMS for emergency helicopter transports. 185-foot structures in the Loudoun County Parkway median would likely be classified as hazards to air navigation under FAA Part 77. Obstruction lighting — high-intensity white strobes during the day, red flashing lights at night — would operate continuously, 24/7, in a residential corridor adjacent to apartment complexes and single-family homes. High-voltage transmission lines are the primary wire-strike hazard for helicopter operations. This could legally necessitate permanent decommissioning of the facility's helicopter access — stripping the county's most populated region of rapid air-bridge access for severe trauma and acute strokes.

Based on FAA Part 77 obstruction standards and Loudoun County EMS medevac designations.

Childcare Facilities

Primrose School of Moorefield Station and Kiddie Academy of Moorefield Station are private childcare centers adjacent to Loudoun County Parkway. Outdoor activity areas would sit directly next to proposed 500kV structures. During the months-long construction phase, heavy crawler cranes and high-voltage conductor stringing would operate directly adjacent to active drop-off zones and outdoor play areas. As private, tuition-based facilities, their viability depends entirely on parental choice. Placement of 500kV lines within feet of classrooms and play areas would effectively destroy these businesses — not through formal taking, but through market reality.

Based on facility locations and proximity to proposed Route 1F corridor.

Gas Station Fire and Explosion Hazard

An Exxon station sits directly across Loudoun County Parkway from the HealthPlex. High-voltage lines induce electric currents on large ungrounded metal objects, creating spark-discharge risk during fuel delivery in an environment saturated with volatile compounds. A downed 500kV conductor onto a fuel station with underground storage tanks represents a catastrophic fire and explosion hazard. A second gas station, currently under construction 1.25 miles south at the intersection of Loudoun County Parkway and Ryan Road, faces identical risks. Dominion's Route 5 is drawn literally through this gas station.

Based on NESC clearance standards and fuel storage facility proximity.

Emergency Access Severing

In the event of severe weather, ice loading, or structural failure, the collapse of a 185-foot tower or downing of live 500kV wires onto Loudoun County Parkway would sever the primary emergency access route — preventing ambulances from reaching the HealthPlex or transferring patients out. The consequences for the 280,000+ residents who depend on this facility are potentially catastrophic.

Based on structural failure scenarios and emergency access routing.

Same "Not Feasible" Claim

The utility told Chino Hills that undergrounding 500kV was impossible. Independent engineers disagreed. The towers came down and underground was built. The same claim is being made in Loudoun. The same is false.

Underground Built Successfully

Despite every infeasibility claim, the underground alternative was built safely, reliably, and on time. Chino Hills is the only U.S. city with operating underground 500kV — and they formally wrote to the Virginia SCC to support LVE's position.

The Regulator Learned a Hard Lesson

The utility started building immediately after approval, betting no one would make them remove towers. They were wrong. The California PUC reversed course — and ordered the utility to build underground at their own expense.

The question is not whether underground can be done. Chino Hills proved it can. The only question is whether our Board and SCC will demand it — before Dominion breaks ground and the leverage is lost.

The Law Is Changing

Virginia's General Assembly Acted — Golden-to-Mars Is the Target

SB 827 and HB 1487 have passed both chambers of the Virginia General Assembly on a bipartisan basis. The legislation was written specifically in response to Golden-to-Mars and creates a pilot program for underground installation. The Board's formal support is now a statutory prerequisite — not a symbolic gesture.

The bill specifically describes a "500 kV and 230 kV line, approximately 8.3 miles long, pending final CPCN approval as of February 1, 2026." That is Golden-to-Mars — by name and description.

Up to 4 Pilot Projects

The SCC may approve up to 4 qualifying underground pilot projects. Golden-to-Mars is eligible as the specifically-described project. The SCC must consider the legislation's intent in its ruling.

The session has concluded — decisive legislative intent the SCC must weigh.

Board Resolution = Statutory Prerequisite

The bill requires local government support to unlock pilot program eligibility. Board inaction forecloses the underground pathway. A resolution supporting the program is required — not symbolic.

The Board has not yet passed this resolution.

Feasibility Already Established in the Record

The bill requires showing technical feasibility. Loudoun County's own independent engineering has already established this in the SCC evidentiary record. The work is done — the Board just needs to act.

County engineers confirmed underground within 500 ft of homes IS feasible.

Cost Structure Is Manageable

The pilot program includes a cost-sharing structure with a residential levy cap. The massive difference in long-term community benefit — versus the permanent harm of overhead towers — makes this a straightforward calculation.

$0.99/month residential levy cap.

The Virginia Mercury called this a nationally-watched precedent. If Virginia's SCC approves overhead lines through residential neighborhoods for data center load — after the legislature specifically directed underground — it will be a landmark decision that affects utility fights across the country.
