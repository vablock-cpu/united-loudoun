Who is Supervisor Saines
here for?
Dominion wants to build 185-foot industrial towers less than 50 feet from homes and schools — to power data centers. Not for reliability. Not for you. This decision is permanent. Once built, these towers stand for generations. What has your supervisor done about it?
Supervisor Koran Saines
Sterling District  ·  Loudoun County Board of Supervisors
Forget leading — he hasn't even shown up. And when he did speak, at the March 17 Board meeting, this is what his constituents heard before they spent the next 90 minutes explaining to him what he could do.
What he told you
Board of Supervisors Meeting · March 17, 2026 · Comments to the gathered community, before public input
"The board has no authority to approve or deny the project."
"The board has no power over the SCC."
"We'll wait patiently, see what happens."
"We're reviewing that"
— on HB 1487, a bill to put power lines underground, already passed by the General Assembly and sitting on the Governor's desk
What he said for them
When colleagues proposed giving the public more say over data center approvals
"Fear-
mongering."
Supervisor Saines accused his own Board colleagues of fear-mongering for suggesting data centers might leave the county if their automatic approvals were restricted.
He couldn't find his voice for your neighborhood.
He had no trouble finding it for theirs.

The state is about to approve this project. Permanently. There are four things the Board can do right now — before that happens.

The data centers aren't sitting this one out.  The industry whose electricity demand created this project hired lawyers, organized, and has been watching this proceeding from day one. They don't need to fight for their outcome. They just need Supervisor Saines to stay out of the way. He has been obliging.

What the Board can do — right now
Supervisor Saines says the Board's hands are tied. They aren't.
The Board has the tools. It just hasn't used them.
1
Take the legal steps already in front of them The Board has options on the table right now that would clear the path for underground power lines — at zero cost to taxpayers. They haven't acted on a single one.
2
Enforce the standard the Board set for itself The Board already said no high-voltage power lines within 500 feet of homes or schools. Now they need to say it again — louder — and mean it.
3
Tell the state and Dominion: no overhead lines through our neighborhoods The Board can file directly with the state commission deciding this case. It has done it before. It should do it now — and make clear it will not support any above-ground high-voltage power lines running through residential communities.
4
Back the bill that puts these lines underground — and make the data centers pay for it HB 1487 would require underground power lines. It's on the Governor's desk right now. The Board can tell the Governor they support it — and signal that the data centers driving this demand should foot the bill, not you.

The data centers are the reason these power lines exist. So why doesn't Supervisor Saines want to make them pay for putting the lines underground?

Sterling District was built for the data center industry. Your neighborhood was an afterthought. Act like you know it.
The clock is running out. On you. For them.
